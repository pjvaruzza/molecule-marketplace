The market

Too big for cylinders. Far too small for a pipeline.

Every hydrogen supply mode is economic only inside a band of daily draw. Our buyers — heat treaters and similar plants at 50–300 kg/day — have outgrown cylinders and MicroBulk by 4× or more, can’t justify a liquid tank or on-site generation, and draw orders of magnitude too little for the pipeline supply that anchors the majors’ business. The one mode that fits, the gaseous tube trailer, is exactly where incumbent economics are weakest — so the band is mispriced or pushed into the wrong mode. That mispriced band is our market.

A buyer “running 2,000 SCFH” across two shifts draws ≈ 75 kg/day — squarely in band.

Why this wins

De-risked by design

Supply is locked before buyers are pursued, and back-to-back pricing means we never carry commodity risk. The business proves itself transaction by transaction rather than betting capital on an unvalidated thesis.

A beachhead, then a template

We win the Gulf Coast first — the densest cluster of byproduct chlor-alkali hydrogen in the country, beside hundreds of heat-treat and industrial buyers — then replicate the playbook region by region. Concentrated liquidity in one market beats thin presence in five.

A moat the majors won't contest

Air Products and Linde have no structural incentive to serve 50–300 kg/day buyers. We own the long tail through operational trust — on-time, on-spec delivery — which compounds into switching costs they can't easily attack.

Distributor margin, broker assets

We're the principal, not a freight broker: we own QA, hazmat compliance, and credit risk, and price like a distributor (~$2.50/kg net take) while owning no plants and leasing no trailers. Drag the net take to $0.50 in the model and watch how thin a pure-brokerage would be.
